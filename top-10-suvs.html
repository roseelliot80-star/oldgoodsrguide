How this list was selected

We reviewed ten distinct new SUVs presented on their U.S. manufacturer websites and selected one specific configuration of each. The list spans compact two-row models and larger three-row options because the right shortlist depends on how many seats you use and what remains available for cargo behind them.

This is an editorial-judgment ranking, not a sales ranking, an instrumented road test, or a safety score. We compared trim-level equipment and price against six practical factors:

  • Entry, exit, and the range of front-seat adjustment;
  • Rearward visibility and the available camera or parking aids;
  • Climate-control and dashboard-control usability;
  • Liftgate operation and cargo access with the relevant seating row raised;
  • Features that may help in a narrow driveway or parking space; and
  • Starting MSRP for the exact reviewed configuration.

Price note: Prices below are manufacturer starting MSRPs researched on November 21, 2026—not dealer quotes, guaranteed transaction prices, or local inventory claims. Destination treatment is identified in each profile. Taxes, registration, options, and dealer charges can change the final price.

Fit is personal. A feature list cannot tell you whether a seat, step-in height, control, or view works for you. Use the profiles to plan an in-person check.

Why the top pick ranks first

The CR-V EX-L covers many everyday bases without requiring the highest trim.

The 2027 Honda CR-V EX-L 2WD ranks first because its manufacturer specification sheet combines a 10-way power driver’s seat with lumbar support, two-position driver-seat memory, a four-way power passenger seat, dual-zone climate control, front and rear parking sensors, and a power tailgate. It also lists 39.3 cubic feet of cargo space behind the raised second row.

That combination directly addresses the questions in this guide at a lower starting MSRP than several more heavily equipped rivals. It does not make the CR-V automatically right for every driver. Its EX-L tailgate is not hands-free, and the ranking does not substitute for checking step-in height, visibility, control reach, or cargo fit in person.

Printable SUV test-drive checklist

1. Entry and exit
I can get into and out of the driver’s seat without an awkward twist, deep drop, or difficult step up.
The door opens wide enough in a space similar to my garage or usual parking spot.
My usual passengers can enter, exit, and reach their belts comfortably.
2. Seat adjustment and control reach
I can adjust the seat height, distance, backrest, and lumbar support to a position I want to keep.
The steering wheel, pedals, shifter, parking brake, and frequently used controls are within comfortable reach.
If two people drive, we have tested seat memory or noted how long readjustment takes.
3. Rearward and side visibility
I have checked the view over my shoulders, in all mirrors, and through the rear window with the headrests in place.
The backup image is clear in daylight; I understand its guidelines and still check my surroundings directly.
If a surround-view camera matters, I have confirmed it is installed on this exact trim—not merely advertised for the model.
4. Narrow-space parking
I have backed into and pulled out of a real space, noting the turning path and where the vehicle’s corners feel.
I can understand the parking-sensor warnings without becoming distracted or startled.
I know which parking aids are standard, optional, or absent on this vehicle.
5. Dashboard and climate controls
While parked, I can adjust temperature, fan speed, defrosters, audio, and phone connections without a confusing menu search.
Text and icons are readable in daylight, and the displays do not create distracting reflections for me.
I understand which connected services require a trial, subscription, or compatible phone.
6. Cargo access
I have loaded my own bags or equipment with the seating rows I normally use still raised.
The liftover height, cargo-floor shape, and seat-folding process work for the items I expect to carry.
The liftgate clears my garage and I can reach its close control; any height-memory or hands-free function has been tested.
7. Price and follow-up
I have an itemized price showing destination, options, taxes, and dealer charges.
I have compared the window sticker with the exact configuration I researched.
